<Warning>
**Work in progress — discussion draft, 21 October 2026.**

This proposal is open for review by prospective partners, contributors, and the
community. It is intended to inform the mainnet policy and subsequent signed
partner agreements. Publication does not activate these terms, create a fee
entitlement, or confirm that a proposed integration is implemented. An adopted
version and its effective date will be published before partners participate
under that policy.
</Warning>

## Purpose

Checkpoint proposes sharing eligible market fees with the protocols whose points
are traded on Checkpoint. The purpose is to support a continuing economic
relationship in which those protocols honour agreed verification, points
recognition, and settlement obligations.

The fee share complements a written agreement. It does not guarantee that points
will receive an allocation, that a token generation event (TGE) will occur, or that
buyers and sellers will avoid losses.

## Two settlement models

The proposed percentages below divide **eligible fees**, not users' points,
purchase proceeds, or token allocations. Checkpoint is listed first in each split.

| Model | Integration and delivery responsibility | Checkpoint share | Participating protocol share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protocol-delivered settlement | Authorised read and write access moves sold points into designated escrow. The partner makes the associated allocation available to the settlement contracts so Checkpoint can execute settlement for users. | 50% | 50% |
| Seller-delivered settlement | Read-only access supports verification. Points remain with the seller, who is responsible for delivering the associated allocation into settlement. | 70% | 30% |

These are proposed fixed categories, not negotiation bands. Eligibility depends
on the settlement capability and commitments provided, rather than access
credentials alone.

### Protocol-delivered settlement

Under this model, sold points are transferred from the seller's account to a
designated escrow address in the participating protocol's points system. The
partner must recognise that transfer and the agreed distribution entitlement
associated with those points.

At TGE or the agreed distribution event, the partner must make the corresponding
assets available to the settlement contracts. This may involve direct delivery
or a compatible claim mechanism. The integration must specify and validate the
route before the market opens.

**Moving points into escrow is not final settlement.** Settlement still depends
on the relevant distribution and delivery of its assets. If those assets are
distributed only to the original sellers, Checkpoint cannot settle on their
behalf without a further delivery step.

The market terms must explain how partial sales, wallet eligibility, multipliers,
bonuses, and allocation caps affect escrowed points. Pooling points into an
address must not silently change the agreed entitlement. The method for
preserving and reconciling that entitlement remains an open design decision.

### Seller-delivered settlement

Under this model, Checkpoint verifies points through read-only access and sellers
remain responsible for delivering the associated allocation into settlement.
The partner's proposed commitments concern reliable verification, recognition
of traded points under the agreed program rules, and cooperation with settlement.

Seller delivery risk remains. The relevant market's deadlines, collateral, and
default rules must be disclosed. Partner fee sharing is not insurance against
seller default.

See [Program Settlement](/architecture/program-settlement) for the currently
documented distribution mechanics. Those mechanics pool the program's delivered
assets and applicable default proceeds for pro-rata claims by escrow-token
holders. This proposal does not establish a separate claim by each buyer against
a particular seller's airdrop.

## Revenue included in the proposal

The proposed starting fee base is **primary settlement fees collected in USDC
from the participating protocol's own market, net of applicable refunds**. The
exact eligible contract events and accounting treatment must be finalised before
adoption. No deduction for general Checkpoint overhead is proposed.

| Revenue or asset | Proposed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible primary settlement fees from a partner market | Shared according to the applicable 50/50 or 70/30 model |
| Revenue from Checkpoint's own points markets | Retained by Checkpoint; no external partner entitlement |
| `fillExtra` escrow-token accrual | Retained by Checkpoint; excluded from partner sharing |
| Portfolio and API subscriptions | Retained by Checkpoint |
| An eventual points-deposit fee | Retained by Checkpoint if introduced |
| Cancellation fees | Open decision; exclusion from partner sharing is proposed |
| Secondary-market trading fees | Separate policy required; user fee and LP, Checkpoint, and partner allocations remain undecided |

Neither primary settlement model includes an LP deduction merely because
settlement occurs. Secondary trades that use liquidity pools require their own
fee schedule. The primary splits do not automatically apply to secondary fees.

For illustration, if a market collects **10,000 USDC in eligible settlement
fees**, the protocol-delivered model allocates 5,000 USDC to each party. The
seller-delivered model allocates 7,000 USDC to Checkpoint and 3,000 USDC to the
participating protocol. These examples do not set the fee charged to traders.

## Written commitments and payment

The proposal requires a written agreement for partner status and fee entitlement
under either model. A market listing or technical adapter alone does not establish
that the points issuer is a partner or has accepted obligations.

A standard agreement, accompanied by a schedule identifying the market or season,
should cover:

- The parties, their authority, and the points program covered.
- The settlement model, integration responsibilities, and allocation rights
  associated with sold points.
- Whether any allocation is committed or remains discretionary.
- Recognition of traded or escrowed points, legitimate corrections, notice of
  material changes, and continuity of verification.
- The applicable fee policy, calculation base, earning conditions, payment
  recipient, and payout schedule.
- Delivery deadlines, reconciliation, disputes, termination, and treatment of
  existing positions if cooperation ends.

The proposed payment condition is successful fulfilment of the relevant
settlement obligations. A TGE announcement or token launch alone would not
qualify. How this condition applies to partial settlement, undisputed amounts,
and individual seller defaults must be resolved before adoption. The payout
interval and verification authority also remain open.

Whether Checkpoint permits markets without a signed issuer agreement is a
separate listing-policy decision. This draft does not establish a fee entitlement
for unsigned issuers.

## Allocation risk and failures

The agreement must distinguish an allocation commitment from a commitment to
treat equivalent points consistently. If an allocation remains discretionary,
points may receive nothing even when the partner honours its commitments.
Excluding Checkpoint positions from a distribution made to otherwise equivalent
points, or failing to deliver an agreed allocation, raises different questions.

The proposed baseline is no adverse treatment solely because points were traded
or moved into authorised escrow. Exact treatment, permitted corrections, and
exceptions must be defined in the market schedule.

Failure procedures must address technical interruptions, incorrect allocations,
payment to the wrong destination, delayed or absent distributions, and disputed
performance. No automatic forfeiture, reimbursement, or compensation entitlement
is established by this draft. Withheld fees cannot replace assets that were never
delivered, and a failed market may generate no settlement fees to fund recovery.

## Administration and future governance

The proposed operating structure is for the Checkpoint Foundation to approve and
oversee the policy, with its operating company administering partner agreements
and fee payments. The contracting entity, approval authority, and delegated
responsibilities must be confirmed against the entities' governing documents.

The launch policy would be adopted through the authorised governance arrangements
in place at that time. A future DAO transition would need to define which powers
transfer and how existing agreements continue to be administered.

The proposed change rule is prospective: new policy versions apply to new markets
or seasons. Existing markets retain their agreed terms for the agreed period,
subject to their documented amendment and emergency provisions. Fee splits,
recipients, and release conditions should be recorded transparently; which rules
will be enforced by smart contracts remains an implementation decision.

## Decisions required before adoption

1. Confirm the eligible fee events, refund treatment, and cancellation-fee policy.
2. Define the allocation associated with sold points and how escrow preserves it.
3. Validate partner delivery and claim routes, including relevant chains.
4. Set payment timing, verification authority, partial-settlement treatment, and
   the dispute process.
5. Set failure, termination, emergency, and any recovery-funding rules.
6. Decide the policy for unsigned issuers and finalise secondary fees before any
   secondary-market feature using those fees is activated.
7. Confirm the contracting entity, adopt the policy, and complete legal and
   technical alignment with the existing user terms and settlement implementation.

Feedback from partners, contributors, and the community should inform these
decisions. Once adopted, the public policy and each signed market schedule should
identify the applicable version and effective date so all parties know the terms
before the market opens.
